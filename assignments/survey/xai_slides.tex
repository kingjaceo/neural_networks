% ----- Slide 1 -----
\begin{frame}{Why XAI?}
  Deep networks are powerful but opaque --- and opacity has consequences.
  \vspace{1em}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item A credit model that can't explain a rejection may violate consumer law
    \item A medical diagnostic system that can't be audited may never be adopted
    \item An autonomous vehicle that can't account for its decisions can't be certified
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{1em}
  After 2019: Transformers took over, LLMs emerged, and the EU AI Act made transparency a \emph{legal} requirement.
\end{frame}

% ----- Slide 2 -----
\begin{frame}{Post-Hoc Explanation Methods}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Feature attribution} --- SHAP, Integrated Gradients, and others unified under the Shapley framework; TreeSHAP made this practical for tree models
    \item \textbf{Counterfactuals} --- ``What would need to change?'' DiCE generates diverse, actionable alternatives; causal variants respect structural causal models
    \item \textbf{Concepts \& prototypes} --- ACE auto-discovers visual concepts; ProtoPNet builds prototypes directly into the architecture
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ----- Slide 3 -----
\begin{frame}{Transformers, LLMs, and Mechanistic Interpretability}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Attention $\neq$ explanation} --- raw attention weights are unreliable; attention rollout and gradient-weighted propagation work better
    \item \textbf{LLMs as explainers} --- chain-of-thought prompting both explains and improves reasoning, but generated rationales can be \emph{plausible} without being \emph{faithful}
    \item \textbf{Mechanistic interpretability} --- reverse-engineers circuits at the weight level; impressive in small models, but scaling to billion-parameter models remains an open problem
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ----- Slide 4 -----
\begin{frame}{Evaluation \& Open Challenges}
  \textbf{Evaluation is arguably the hardest unsolved problem in XAI.}
  \vspace{0.8em}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Faithfulness vs.\ plausibility: these desiderata frequently conflict
    \item Application-, human-, and functionally-grounded evaluations often disagree
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{0.8em}
  \textbf{Emerging challenges:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Explanations can be adversarially manipulated to conceal discrimination
    \item Transparency facilitates membership inference attacks --- privacy and explainability pull in opposite directions
    \item Most XAI methods don't scale to foundation models
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
